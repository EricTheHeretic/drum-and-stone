# Drum & Stone — Ramen notes

South Fork, Pennsylvania. Drum & Stone LLC. October 6, 2026.

There is no earlier ramen PDF in the repo or in the stored project notes. This file is the consolidation.

## What was decided

- The product on the site is labeled Ramen, not Dry noodles.
- It is a dry, shelf-stable noodle, not fresh ramen and not a refrigerated soup kit.
- It is one of the first products on the Pennsylvania Limited Food Establishment plan: roasted coffee, shelf-stable chocolate, ramen, dried fruit, beef jerky, and biltong.
- Nothing is sold until the LFE registration is approved and product liability insurance is in place.
- Plan review goes to RA-AGPLANREVIEW@pa.gov at least 60 days before production.
- The live site is an order note only until that approval.

## Why dry, not fresh

A Limited Food Establishment kitchen can make non-potentially-hazardous food that does not need refrigeration after it is made. Fresh noodles and broth are the wrong product for that license. The pack is the dry noodle. Broth, if offered later, is a separate decision and is not part of this product.

## Broth and vegetables

No broth recipe or vegetable list was stored. The picture on the site is a cooked bowl. That is not the pack.

What cannot go in the pack under this license: fresh broth, meat stock, fresh-cut vegetables, cooked vegetables, garlic in oil, or anything that has to be refrigerated.

What can be considered, still unchosen: a dry seasoning packet, and vegetables that are already dry when they go in the bag. Dried mushroom, dried scallion, dried seaweed. Salt, dried ginger, dried garlic, chili. Soy only if the allergen line says so. No amounts are set.

The customer boils water. The packet is not a jar of broth.

## What still has to be written down before the application

- Flour or blend, and whether any egg is used. Egg makes the review harder. Start with an eggless wheat noodle.
- Kansui or alkaline salt, if used, and the amount.
- Target dry shape: straight, wavy, or nest.
- Dry time and temperature, and how the batch is cooled before packing.
- Pack size and the shelf-life claim. Do not print a date until a batch has been held and checked.
- Allergen line. Wheat is expected. Egg, soy, and sesame only if they are actually in the batch.
- Water activity. Dried fruit and jerky already need a lab water activity and pH. Dry ramen should get a water activity number too, so the packet can sit on a shelf without a refrigerator.

## Label, once the recipe is fixed

Product name, Drum & Stone, South Fork, Pennsylvania, net weight, ingredients in descending order, allergen statement, and a lot code. No health claim. No "cooks in three minutes" until a timed test is written down.

## Open

Recipe, alkali level, dry schedule, pack size, seasoning, dried vegetables, and the lab water activity are not set. This note does not invent them.
